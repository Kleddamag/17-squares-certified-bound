# Draft: global translation-to-basis reduction

This is a self-contained proof draft for a reduction intended to cover all physical packings. Independent mathematical review remains pending. It does not prove a bound beyond the accepted baseline or identify the optimum. The accompanying rational experiment checks one instance, not the quantified theorem below.

## Physical formulation, with all boundaries retained

A unit square with center p and oriented orthonormal axes u,v is p+[-1/2,1/2]u+[-1/2,1/2]v. Its support radius in direction n is (|n.u|+|n.v|)/2. Choosing an intrinsic quarter-turn representative changes neither the square nor its center. Thus take every angle in [0,pi/2] and relabel the 17 indistinguishable squares so their angles are ordered. Equal angles and both duplicate endpoints are allowed. No individual reflection or common-angle assumption is made.

Put u_i=(c_i,s_i), v_i=(-s_i,c_i), with c_i,s_i>=0. Containment in [0,L]^2 is equivalent to the four inequalities x_i>=h_i, y_i>=h_i, L-x_i>=h_i, L-y_i>=h_i, h_i=(c_i+s_i)/2.

Two squares have disjoint interiors exactly when some signed axis n in {+/-u_i,+/-v_i,+/-u_j,+/-v_j} satisfies

    n.(p_j-p_i) >= r_i(n)+r_j(n).

Here is a justification of the axis list, including degeneracy. Overlap of interiors is equivalent to p_j-p_i lying in the interior of the Minkowski sum of the two centered squares. This sum is a centrally symmetric polygon. A support face of a sum is the sum of its support faces; an edge therefore has a normal which is an edge normal of at least one summand. All its facet normals occur in the stated list. Membership in the polygon's interior requires strict satisfaction of every facet inequality, so absence of interior overlap gives a weak separating inequality in this list. Conversely any listed weak inequality separates the open interiors. Coincident orientations merely merge some facets and duplicate some alternatives.

For ordered angles i<j, every listed direction has the same support sum

    b_ij=(1+cos(theta_j-theta_i)+sin(theta_j-theta_i))/2.

Indeed the two cross-axis absolute dot products are cos(diff) and sin(diff), both nonnegative for diff in [0,pi/2]. A square's own axis radius is 1/2. This is also directly checked, independently via vertex projections, in the rational experiment.

For fixed independent angles choose ONE valid alternative sigma_ij for each of the 136 pairs. The 68 wall rows and these 136 pair rows define P={z:Az>=b}, with z=(x_1,y_1,...,x_17,y_17,L) in R^35. Every point of P is a physical packing. No converse for this particular separator selection is needed.

## Attainment and an optimal vertex

If P contains a point with side U, intersect it with L<=U. Since h_i>=1/2, the walls imply 1<=L<=U and h_i<=x_i,y_i<=L-h_i. This intersection is nonempty, closed, and bounded. Thus L attains its minimum m on P: any smaller point would already be in this sublevel. The optimal face F=P intersect {L=m} is nonempty and compact.

A compact polyhedron has an extreme point without a nondegeneracy assumption: successively minimize each of its 35 coordinate functions, always on the remaining compact optimal face. At the end all coordinates are fixed, so the final point z* is unique. If it were a proper midpoint of two points of the original F, both endpoints would successively have every minimizing coordinate and hence equal z*. Thus z* is extreme in F. It is extreme in P too, since both endpoints of a midpoint in P must have L=m when their average has the globally minimal L.

At a vertex, the active row normals span R^35. Otherwise a nonzero vector d annihilates every active normal. Finitely many inactive rows have strictly positive slacks, so sufficiently small z*+epsilon*d and z*-epsilon*d are both feasible. This contradicts extremality. This proof applies to lower-dimensional P and degenerate vertices.

## A nonnegative dual supported on a full basis

Let c=e_L and C be the cone of active row normals. First, a conic representation can always be reduced to linearly independent generators. If positively weighted generators are dependent, choose a nonzero relation beta, changing its sign so some beta_k>0. Subtract tau*beta from the weights, where tau=min_{beta_k>0}(lambda_k/beta_k). All weights stay nonnegative and at least one positive weight disappears. Repeat. Consequently C is a finite union of cones generated by independent subsets. Each such cone is closed: its coefficient map is a linear isomorphism from its image span, and its nonnegative orthant is closed. Hence C is closed.

If c is outside C, let q be its nearest point in C. Existence follows by restricting to a sufficiently large closed bounded ball. The projection inequality (c-q).(a-q)<=0 holds for every a in C. Using a=0 and a=2q gives (c-q).q=0; using scaled active generators gives (c-q).a_k<=0. Therefore d=q-c satisfies a_k.d>=0 on every active row and c.d=-||c-q||^2<0. For small positive epsilon the inactive slacks still remain nonnegative, so z*+epsilon*d is feasible and decreases L, a contradiction. Thus c is in C.

Reduce its conic representation to independent active normals as above. Extend that independent set to 35 independent active normals, possible by the spanning property. Assign zero weight to the added rows. This yields a full-rank active basis B with lambda>=0 and A_B^T lambda=c. Since A_B z*=b_B,

    z*=A_B^-1 b_B,   lambda=A_B^-T c,
    L(z)>=lambda.b_B=L(z*) for every z in P.

Zero weights, redundant contacts, rattlers, and singular alternatives have not been excluded. We invert only this full-rank B at the actual angle value, never a generically chosen singular basis.

## Global implication and finite angle cases

Start from ANY physical packing, not a numerical neighborhood. Select valid separators and apply the preceding argument at its unchanged 17 angles. The resulting basis packing has side no larger. The physical feasible set under the verified feasible cap is compact: centers, side and canonical angles range over closed bounded sets, walls are closed, and each pair constraint is a finite union of closed inequalities. Hence a global minimum is attained and one of these basis packings attains it too.

To enumerate bases without first enumerating all 8^136 complete separator selections, choose 35 rows from the universal inventory of 68 walls and 8 alternatives per pair, with at most one row from each pair. Every basis in the preceding proof has this form. For a candidate basis, require full rank, nonnegative lambda, all wall constraints, and at least one of the eight physical alternatives for every pair at its reconstructed common centers. Any such point is a physical packing; omitted pair selections can be filled by their valid alternatives. Thus these basis-angle cases cover at least one global minimizer. They need not contain every original center arrangement, since replacing centers was an intentional dominating step.

The half-angle substitution t_i=tan(theta_i/2) gives independent ordered parameters 0<=t_1<=...<=t_17<=1 and rational c_i,s_i. All factors 1+t_i^2 are positive. Rational inversion introduces determinant denominators; their signs must be split or handled without unsafe multiplication. A singular value for one basis is covered by another nonsingular active basis, including equal-angle/endpoint values. No branch may be discarded on a genericity argument.

For an exact feasible endpoint alpha, the missing global lower lemma is: every candidate basis and admissible angle value either has L_B>=alpha or violates a wall or the full eight-alternative disjunction for some pair. Proving this statement for the entire finite family, with exact continuous angle coverage, would imply the matching lower bound. No enumeration or pruning theorem for that family is supplied here. Selected projection equalities need not be physical contacts, so graph planarity and observed contact structure are not automatically valid pruning rules.
